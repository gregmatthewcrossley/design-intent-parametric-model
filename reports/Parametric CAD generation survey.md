# Build the verifier before the model

> **Correction (2026-11-16).** From the fresh-eyes review (NOV-03); the body is left as written. "Nobody measures intent" and "no accepted metric measures design intent" hold for the recent ML literature on CAD generation, whose benchmarks measure shape similarity. They do not hold for CAD research and education: semantic feature modelling (Bidarra & Bronsvoort 2000), valid-parameter-range work (Hoffmann & Kim 2001; Joan-Arinyo & Mata 2001) and design-intent rubrics (Company, Contero, Camba, Otey, 2015–2019) already address it. See [`docs/architecture.md` §1.1](../docs/architecture.md#11-prior-art-and-whats-actually-new).

As of November 2026, the strongest evidence-based path to an "LLM for parametric CAD" runs through **code-as-CAD**. A pretrained (vision-)language model emits an executable program, a real kernel runs it, and the kernel's verdict feeds back as a repair signal or an RL reward. Models trained from scratch on command tokens have been overtaken. **Small open models (1.5–7B) fine-tuned with SFT and then RL on geometric rewards** dominate reconstruction from point clouds and images: cadrille reaches about 90% IoU and 0% invalid outputs on DeepCAD. Prompted **frontier models with kernel-error retries** beat every purpose-trained model on human-written text prompts, but still reach only about 0.5–0.6 IoU on easy parts and fail 65%+ of the time on sweeps, lofts and shells. The binding constraints are not compute. They are four other things. There is **no commercially clean, feature-rich dataset**. Almost all strong artifacts are **non-commercial**. **Persistent topological references** for fillets, chamfers and shells are unsolved in learned systems. And **no accepted metric measures design intent** (meaningful parameters, constraints, edit stability). For a solo builder, the evidence points to a clear first version. Define a small, typed, kernel-neutral sketch+feature DSL. Compile it to OpenCascade (build123d/CadQuery) for a fast local verification loop. Wrap a frontier model in a spec → generate → compile → check → repair agent. Build your own procedural data and an edit-stability benchmark. Defer fine-tuning an open 2–3B VLM until that harness exists. The DSL, the compiler and the benchmark are the parts most likely to be useful to others. The model is the part most likely to be superseded.

## Code output plus RL rewards overtook token models in under two years

The field moved in two clear steps. Through 2024, the reference systems were transformers trained from scratch over **DeepCAD-style command tokens**. DeepCAD itself had 6 command types, a fixed 16-slot parameter vector, 8-bit quantization and a 60-command cap, and it discarded every model that used anything but sketch+extrude ([DeepCAD](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.09492)). Text2CAD (NeurIPS 2024) attached text prompts at four skill levels to about 170K of those models and trained a 363M from-scratch transformer ([Text2CAD](https://github.com/SadilKhan/Text2CAD)). From late 2024 the center of gravity moved to **pretrained LLMs emitting CadQuery Python**. CAD-Recode put a small point-cloud projector in front of Qwen2-1.5B. It trained only on **1M procedurally generated CadQuery programs** and reported "10× lower Chamfer Distance and over 20% higher IoU" than prior work ([CAD-Recode](https://cad-recode.github.io)). Its own ablation shows that the procedural data, not the human data, drives most of the gain: training on DeepCAD-160k instead drops Fusion360 IoU from 87.8 to 67.6 ([CAD-Recode](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.14042)).

The representation effect is unusually clean. In Text-to-CadQuery, a **124M Python-pretrained CodeGPT beat the 363M Text2CAD transformer** on every accuracy metric. GPT-2 medium, which is not code-pretrained, did not improve further, so the authors attribute the gain to Python pretraining priors. Fine-tuning took under an hour for 124M and 33 hours for 7B on one A100, while Text2CAD took two days from scratch ([Text-to-CadQuery](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.06507)). Code output also makes results legible to general LLMs: GPT-4o answered **76.5% of CAD-QA questions** from CAD-Recode's CadQuery, versus 63.2% from a token-sequence baseline ([CAD-Recode](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.14042)). Text2CAD-Bench (May 2026) confirms that CadQuery beats DeepCAD-style command sequences as an output format for every LLM it tested ([Text2CAD-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.18430v1)). Hybrids exist. CAD-Llama converts DeepCAD commands into a Python-like hierarchical DSL with semantic component names, and CADmium keeps JSON but uses a code LLM. Both beat Text2CAD ([CAD-Llama](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.04481v2), [CADmium](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.09792v3)).

The second ingredient is **reinforcement learning from kernel feedback**, and every paper that tried it after SFT reports large gains. cadrille (ICLR 2026 Oral) fine-tunes Qwen2-VL-2B on point clouds, multi-view images or text. It then runs online RL ("Dr. CPPO") with a deliberately crude reward: **10 × IoU, or −10 if the code fails**. That lifts DeepCAD point-cloud IoU from 87.1 to 90.2 and cuts invalid outputs from 2.1% to 0.0%. On real scans (CC3D), IoU rises from 61.8 to 67.9 and invalidity falls from 5.9% to 0.2% ([cadrille](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22914)). Offline DPO cut invalidity but "barely changes accuracy" ([cadrille v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22914v3)). CAD-Coder (NeurIPS 2025) does text→CadQuery on Qwen2.5-7B with chain-of-thought and GRPO. It shows that **RL needs the SFT prior**: GRPO without SFT reached mean CD 76.20, about the same as SFT alone (74.55), while SFT+CoT+GRPO reached 6.54. It also found that **8K high-quality SFT samples beat 70K medium-quality ones** ([CAD-Coder](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/564a224e88f2490f3c1deaae877d37e3-Paper-Conference.pdf)). Image-to-code followed the same recipe through 2025–2026 (ReCAD, GACO-CAD, CAD-RL, CME-CAD, ConCAD), with IoU-thresholded rewards and length or conciseness terms ([GACO-CAD](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17157), [awesome-ai4cad](https://github.com/gudo7208/awesome-ai4cad)).

The most important structural fact about this recipe is that **geometric rewards need only a target shape, not a paired program**. cadrille's RL stage uses unannotated DeepCAD and Fusion360 meshes and keeps only "hard" examples ([cadrille](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22914)). Any licensable mesh or STEP corpus therefore becomes RL training data. The cost sits in kernel execution: CAD-Coder's GRPO took **146 hours on 8× A800 versus 7 hours for SFT**, and the authors observed reward hacking on overlapping features ([CAD-Coder](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/564a224e88f2490f3c1deaae877d37e3-Paper-Conference.pdf)).

Engineering drawings are the least mature input, which is ironic because they are the only one that reliably carries absolute dimensions. CAD-MLLM names missing dimensions in text as a core failure ([CAD-MLLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04954v1)). The verified drawing work is narrow. PlankAssembly and CAD2Program cover cabinets from orthographic views ([PlankAssembly](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/html/Hu_PlankAssembly_Robust_3D_Reconstruction_from_Three_Orthographic_Views_with_Learnt_ICCV_2023_paper.html), [CAD2Program](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.11892)). Drawing2CAD (ACM MM 2025, MIT-licensed, 150K+ vector-drawing/CAD pairs) improves on a DeepCAD-vector baseline only marginally (MCD 11.98 vs 12.15) ([Drawing2CAD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.18733), [GitHub](https://github.com/lllssc/Drawing2CAD)). Several 2026 LLM drawing papers exist (Drawing-Recode, Ortho2CAD, SOV-CAD, IterCAD), but no surveyed work shows robust extraction of dimension callouts or GD&T from real PDFs ([awesome-ai4cad](https://github.com/gudo7208/awesome-ai4cad)).

## Each of the three paradigms wins on a different axis

The evidence does not crown one approach. It shows that **each paradigm wins in a different regime**, and the regimes can be told apart by how far the input is from the training distribution.

| | (a) Fine-tune open (V)LM, SFT + RL | (b) Agent around frontier model | (c) Train custom model from scratch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Best evidence | cadrille: ~90% IoU, 0% IR on DeepCAD points/images ([cadrille](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22914)) | GPT-5.2 leads Text2CAD-Bench L1–L3 on CD/IoU; IoU 0.59 at L1 ([Text2CAD-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.18430v1)) | BrepARG/TG-Diff reach ~86–88% B-rep validity on DeepCAD ([BrepARG](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.16771v2), [TG-Diff](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.21928)) |
| Where it fails | Out of distribution: Text2CADQuery IoU 0.02–0.07 on human prompts; no generalization to unseen part families ([Text2CAD-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.18430v1), [BenchCAD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10865)) | Advanced features: >65% IR at L3 for most models; validity gains do not lift judged quality ([Text2CAD-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.18430v1), [RealCADBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03773)) | Sequence models limited to sketch+extrude; B-rep models have no history or constraints ([DeepCAD](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.09492)) |
| Compute | SFT ≤3B: 1 GPU, hours to a day; RL: 8× H100/A800, days ([Text-to-CadQuery](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.06507), [CAD-Coder](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/564a224e88f2490f3c1deaae877d37e3-Paper-Conference.pdf)) | API cost per attempt; "several minutes" per attempt in practice ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47411437)) | 4 GPUs for 1–3 days at 82–300M params ([BrepARG](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.16771v2), [TG-Diff](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.21928)) |
| Main bottleneck | Paired data and a fast kernel gym | Strategy errors, API hallucination, cost/latency | Licensed data in your representation; forfeits language priors |

**Fine-tuning wins in-distribution and on geometric inputs.** For point clouds and rendered images of DeepCAD-like parts, fine-tuned 2B VLMs are effectively state of the art. On the original Text2CAD test prompts, CAD-Coder's fine-tuned 7B reached mean CD×10³ 6.54 with 1.45% invalid, while zero-shot GPT-4o produced invalid code 70.5% of the time and Claude-3.7-Sonnet 47% ([CAD-Coder](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/564a224e88f2490f3c1deaae877d37e3-Paper-Conference.pdf)). Those prompts, however, are machine-generated descriptions of DeepCAD sequences. On **600 human-curated tasks** in Text2CAD-Bench the ranking flips. GPT-5.2 with three kernel-error retries reached IoU 0.59 at L1, while from-scratch Text2CAD scored 0.08 and the fine-tuned Text2CADQuery and CADFusion scored 0.07 and 0.03, often producing **valid but wrong geometry** ([Text2CAD-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.18430v1)). BenchCAD's 106 industrial part families confirm that "RL and fine-tuning do not generalize to unseen families" ([BenchCAD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10865)). Out-of-distribution fragility also appears in reverse engineering. In CADFit's harder-mesh evaluation, cadrille collapsed to IoU 0.092 on Fusion360 while CAD-Recode held 0.761 ([CADFit](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.01171v1)). That contradicts cadrille's self-reported 85.0 and is most likely a protocol difference, but it is a warning that RL-tuned models can overfit their reward distribution.

**Agents win on breadth but plateau on strategy.** Kernel-error retry and visual self-critique help consistently but modestly. CADCodeVerify's render-and-question loop moved GPT-4 from 92.0% to 94.0% success and cut point-cloud distance 11–18%, with weaker models gaining more. An image-only feedback baseline sometimes made results *worse* ([CADCodeVerify](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.05340v1)). In RealCADBench's 12,632 industrial tasks, six frontier models writing FreeCAD Python reached only 0.284–0.538 Solid IoU. Wrapping GPT-5.5 in a Codex agent raised executability and IoU on assemblies but **lowered the rubric-judge score by 6.98 points** ([RealCADBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03773)). The near-perfect agentic result comes from adding structure, not more retries. ReliCAD converts requirements into an explicit spec of relations, dependencies and feature order, drives a kernel through an "agent-ready API" with constraint solving, and localizes and repairs errors. It reports **99.8% validity and IoU 0.875** on HistCAD ([ReliCAD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22325)). CADTests goes further: executable requirement tests act both as the metric and as generation guidance, and simple test-guided baselines beat existing methods ([CADTests](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07807)). The two paradigms are also converging. TOOLCAD trains open models in a CAD "gym" with SFT plus online curriculum RL and claims parity with proprietary agents ([TOOLCAD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07960)).

**Training from scratch is now a B-rep research track, not a parametric one.** Since 2024, from-scratch effort has moved to direct B-rep generation: BrepGen, DTGBrepGen, AutoBrep, BrepARG and TG-Diff, with DeepCAD validity rising to about 85–88% ([BrepARG](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.16771v2), [TG-Diff](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.21928)). These models output "dumb" solids with no history or constraints. Spectral's commercial SGS-1 shows the practical result: STEP output, non-tangent fillets, slightly warped planes, and an "unusable" multi-pocket housing ([getleo.ai](https://www.getleo.ai/blog/text-to-cad-tools-comparison-guide)). The from-scratch sequence models (DeepCAD, SkexGen, HNC-CAD) are small and cheap but lose to code LLMs on every head-to-head benchmark, and they discard pretrained knowledge of language, units and engineering vocabulary. For this project, the only defensible from-scratch component is a **narrow auxiliary model**, such as a sketch-constraint inferencer in the Vitruvion/AutoConstrain mold, rather than the main generator.

## Data, not compute, is the wall, and it is mostly non-commercial

A solo builder can afford the compute in this field. SFT of a 1.5–3B code VLM fits on one 80GB GPU in hours to a day, and LoRA on 24–48GB cards is plausible ([Text-to-CadQuery](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.06507), [cadrille v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22914v3)). What cannot be bought is **real, feature-rich, permissively licensed construction histories**.

| Dataset | Content | License / status |
|---|---|---|
| ABC | ~1M Onshape models incl. STEP, Parasolid and original FeatureScript | Creators keep copyright; points to Onshape ToU ([ABC](https://deep-geometry.github.io/abc-dataset/)) |
| DeepCAD | ~170–178K sketch+extrude sequences from ABC | Code MIT; data license unverified ([DeepCAD](https://github.com/ChrisWu1997/DeepCAD)) |
| SketchGraphs | 15M Onshape sketches as constraint graphs | Code MIT; data copyright with creators ([SketchGraphs](https://github.com/PrincetonLIPS/SketchGraphs)) |
| Fusion 360 Gallery | 8,625 human sketch+extrude sequences + Gym | **Non-commercial research only**, no redistribution ([LICENSE](https://github.com/AutodeskAILab/Fusion360GalleryDataset/blob/master/LICENSE.md)) |
| Text2CAD | 4-level text on DeepCAD; 605 GB with renders | **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0**, gated ([HF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/SadilKhan/Text2CAD)) |
| CAD-Recode v1.5 | ~1M procedural CadQuery programs | **CC BY-NC 4.0** ([HF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/filapro/cad-recode-v1.5/blame/main/README.md)) |
| CADFS (May 2026) | ~451K real Onshape designs as verified FeatureScript, 15 ops | No license stated ([CADFS](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.01925)) |
| Zero-to-CAD (Apr 2026) | ~1M agent-synthesized programs beyond sketch+extrude; 100K released | Not verified ([Zero-to-CAD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24479)) |
| ARCADE-1.5M (Oct 2026) | 1.5M programs up to 150+ ops; 12.5M state–action steps | Release not stated ([StepCAD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03799)) |
| BenchCAD / Text2CAD-Bench / CADPrompt | 17.9K / 600 / 200 eval tasks in CadQuery | Licenses not stated ([BenchCAD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10865), [Text2CAD-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.18430v1), [CADPrompt](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.05340v2)) |

The Onshape-derived corpora sit in a genuine gray zone. Onshape's Terms of Use (§1(g)(ii)) grant third parties a worldwide license to use IP in free-plan public documents "without restriction". But documents with a LICENSE tab can reserve rights, the terms say nothing about ML training, and §4(b)(9) **forbids scraping and data mining**, so building a fresh crawl is off the table ([Onshape ToU](https://www.onshape.com/en/legal/terms-of-use)). Curators' added layers bind independently: text annotations, renders and synthetic programs under CC BY-NC(-SA). Model artifacts follow the same pattern. **cadrille (Apache-2.0 code, HF weights), CADmium (Apache-2.0 models), Point2CAD (Apache-2.0)** and the MIT group (DeepCAD, SkexGen, SketchGraphs code, AutoBrep, BrepDiff, Point2Cyl, SECAD-Net, Drawing2CAD, FlexCAD) are reusable. CAD-Recode, Text2CAD, HNC-CAD, BrepGen and BRepNet are research-only, and several popular repos (Vitruvion, GenCAD, DTGBrepGen, CAD-Coder, Text-to-CadQuery) have no license file at all, which by default grants no reuse rights ([cadrille](https://github.com/col14m/cadrille), [CADmium](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.09792v3), [AutoBrep](https://github.com/AutodeskAILab/AutoBrep), [hnc-cad](https://github.com/samxuxiang/hnc-cad), [BrepGen](https://github.com/samxuxiang/BrepGen)). One caveat: cadrille's weights were trained on CAD-Recode and Text2CAD data, so the provenance of the "permissive" weights is itself mixed.

The escape hatch is that **synthetic data works**, and anyone can regenerate it. CAD-Recode's generator is simple: random circles and rotated rectangles, unioned or cut, extruded on canonical planes, quantized, validated with `BRepCheck_Analyzer` and deduplicated. Yet it beat human data ([CAD-Recode](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.14042)). Zero-to-CAD trains a 2B VLM purely on agentically synthesized programs and beats GPT-5.2 on multi-view image→CAD ([Zero-to-CAD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24479)). Text-to-CadQuery annotated 170K samples with Gemini 2.0 Flash, using an execute-and-fix loop, for **$170 in 9 hours** ([Text-to-CadQuery](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.06507)). The limit is coverage and realism. Synthetic SFT alone drops from 87% IoU on DeepCAD to about 60% on real scans, and naive mixing of procedural and human data hurt cadrille's SFT ([cadrille v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22914v3)). CADFS's ablations show that real-data diversity, a richer 15-op vocabulary and better annotations each add measurable gains: 40% better CD than cadrille on text-conditioned DeepCAD generation ([CADFS](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.01925)). Synthetic data, in other words, gets you executable fluency. It does not get you the distribution of things engineers actually make.

## Your DSL must solve the references that every learned system has dodged

The literature gives a surprisingly specific design brief for a neutral sketch+feature DSL, partly through what it avoided. DeepCAD left fillets out because they "need B-rep reference" ([DeepCAD](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.09492)). A 2025 survey notes that generators use extrusion precisely "to avoid the direct generation of B-rep" ([MDPI survey](https://mdpi.com/2076-3417/15/12/6681)). As a result, nearly every published model emits **sketch+extrude only**, and frontier models asked for sweeps, lofts and twist-extrudes quietly substitute extrudes ([BenchCAD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10865)). Holes and patterns become N repeated cut-extrudes, which erases the hole spec and the pattern count that carry design intent. The 2026 frontier is just starting to cross this line. Pointer-CAD lets an LLM select B-rep edges and faces by pointer for fillets and chamfers ([Pointer-CAD](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.04337)). CADFit recovers fillets and chamfers by post-hoc line search ([CADFit](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.01171v1)). CADFS and ARCADE span extrude, revolve, sweep, loft, fillet, chamfer, shell, hole, patterns and mirror ([CADFS](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.01925), [StepCAD](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.03799)).

Several design rules follow directly from that evidence:

- **Express topological references as semantic queries tied to the creating feature, not raw indices.** For example: "convex edges created by extrude 3", or "the top face of boss". This is the FeatureScript query model, and it is what keeps references stable when a parameter changes the face count. Index pointers (SolidGen, AutoBrep, Pointer-CAD) work for one-shot generation but are exactly what breaks on regeneration ([SolidGen](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.13944), [AutoBrep](https://github.com/AutodeskAILab/AutoBrep)).
- **Make constraints first-class and checkable at compile time.** Real sketches are mostly under-constrained, so imitation learning reproduces under-constraint. Vitruvion fully constrained only 8.87% of sketches and SFT reached 34.24%. Solver-feedback RL (RLOO/GRPO) raised that to **91.6–93.1%** ([AutoConstrain](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13178)). A compiler that reports remaining degrees of freedom turns this into both a lint and a reward.
- **Carry real units, named parameters and equations in the DSL.** Treat 8-bit quantization in a normalized frame as a training-time encoding only. Quantization helped DeepCAD learn (CD 0.752 vs 2.142 for regression), but it destroys the dimensional semantics that make a model parametric ([DeepCAD](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.09492)). CAD-Llama's gain from semantic component names supports keeping the surface readable ([CAD-Llama](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.04481v2)).
- **Keep the surface syntax close to Python or CadQuery.** That lets the model inherit code pretraining. The 124M-beats-363M result is the strongest single argument here, and frontier models already write CadQuery and FreeCAD Python fluently ([Text-to-CadQuery](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.06507)). Their characteristic failure is hallucinated API calls and wrong argument orders ([texocad](https://blog.texocad.ai/posts/freecad-ai-macro)). A small, strictly typed, well-documented DSL is easier to constrain-decode and validate than a sprawling general API.
- **Keep exact analytic primitives.** VQ/UV-grid B-rep tokens turn a cylinder into an approximate patch. ParaCAD's 2026 return to exact surface types points the same way ([ParaCAD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17093)).

On the backend, the practical answer is **OpenCascade via build123d or CadQuery** for the inner loop. It installs with pip or conda, runs headless, batches in a worker pool with per-sample timeouts, and is what CAD-Recode, cadrille, CADPrompt and BenchCAD all use ([CAD-Recode](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.14042.pdf), [BenchCAD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10865)). Onshape FeatureScript is the most faithful target for "real" feature trees, and CADFS shows it can represent 451K real designs. But Onshape's API caps standard and education plans at **2,500 calls per year** (10,000 for EDU Enterprise) and rejects requests once the cap is hit ([Onshape API limits](https://onshape-public.github.io/docs/auth/limits)). That rules it out for RL and makes it an export target, not a training loop. Zoo's KCL is the closest commercial analogue to this project's DSL, but its geometry engine is proprietary and cloud-hosted ([Zoo FAQ](https://zoo.dev/docs/faq)). The precedent for a vendor-neutral command layer with per-system "macro" translation is the older Macro-Parametrics TransCAD project ([TransCAD](https://github.com/Macro-parametrics/TransCAD)). Treat the license details for OCCT (LGPL with exception), CadQuery and build123d (Apache-2.0), and FreeCAD's planegcs and SolveSpace solvers as background knowledge to confirm before shipping. The notes did not verify them against primary sources.

## Benchmarks measure shape similarity; nobody measures intent

The standard protocol is **median Chamfer distance (×10³), volumetric IoU and invalidity ratio** on the DeepCAD, Fusion360 and CC3D test splits ([cadrille](https://github.com/col14m/cadrille)). Those benchmarks are near saturation: DeepCAD median CD ≈ 0.16–0.17 and IoU 90–92%. The 2026 papers have moved to harder sets: ABC-Hard, CADENA-Bench, BenchCAD, Text2CAD-Bench, RealCADBench ([CADFit](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.01171v1), [CADENA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00799)). Cross-paper numbers are treacherous. CAD-Recode's self-reported CC3D IoU of 74.2 uses test-time sampling, and cadrille re-runs it at 60.5 ([CAD-Recode](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.14042), [cadrille](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22914)). BrepGen's validity is 68.2% in one paper and 50.5% in another ([BrepARG](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.16771v2), [TG-Diff](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.21928)). Image-to-CAD IoU normalizations differ ([GIFT](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.27448)). No public leaderboard reconciles them.

More fundamentally, Text2CAD-Bench shows that **executability, geometric similarity and feature-level correctness are "largely independent"**. GPT-5.2 leads on CD at L1–L3 yet scores lowest on the L4 feature-level judge, and execution rate "overstates performance" ([Text2CAD-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.18430v1)). CADFS broadens geometry metrics to topology, adding edge Chamfer distance, dangling edges, self-intersection and flux enclosure ([CADFS](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.01925)). CADTests reframes evaluation as requirement-level unit tests ([CADTests](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07807)). But this survey found **no benchmark for edit stability**: perturb each parameter by ±k%, rebuild, then check validity, topology preservation and reference survival. It also found no quantitative work on topological-naming failures, long histories (more than about 30 features), GD&T or manufacturability. AutoConstrain's constraint-completeness metric is the only quantitative design-intent measure, and it covers 2D sketches only ([AutoConstrain](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13178)). For a project whose name is "design intent", this gap is the opportunity. An edit-stability and requirement-test benchmark over a neutral DSL would be novel, cheap to build on OCCT, and useful to every group in the field.

## Incumbents ship assistants; only code-first startups ship parametric generation

The commercial picture confirms the technical one. As of November 2026, the shipped products that turn prompts into **editable parametric** models are both code-first. **Zoo's Zookeeper** (January 2026) writes and executes KCL and returns a feature tree, with mass and volume checks, an MCP server and an enterprise pilot that converts customers' CAD into KCL for fine-tuning ([Zoo](https://docs.zoo.dev/research/zookeeper), [Zoo enterprise](https://zoo.dev/enterprise)). **Adam** (YC W25, $4.1M seed) generates CadQuery/OpenSCAD ([mezha.net](https://mezha.net/eng/news/adam-startup-raises-4-1m-to-revolutionize-ai-cad-modeling/)). Hands-on reviews by a competitor with a conflict of interest find both good on brackets and housings. They fail on snap-fit enclosures, organic surfaces and Booleans on non-intersecting bodies ([getleo.ai](https://www.getleo.ai/blog/text-to-cad-tools-comparison-guide)). The incumbents ship assistants:

- **SOLIDWORKS AURA** is a documentation and conversational assistant that "doesn't create parts from text" ([texocad](https://blog.texocad.ai/posts/solidworks-aura-ai)).
- **Onshape AI Advisor** explicitly does not generate designs ([Onshape](https://www.onshape.com/en/blog/ai-advisor-artificial-intelligence-cad-engineering-software)).
- **Siemens NX Design Copilot** is a help assistant ([getleo.ai](https://www.getleo.ai/blog/built-in-ai-cad-plm-comparison-2026)).
- **Autodesk** has shipped AutoConstrain. Fusion AutoTimeline (solid → feature tree), AutoAssemble and neural CAD, which grew out of Project Bernini, are announced without dates ([Engineering.com](https://www.engineering.com/au-2026-autodesks-ai-assistant-is-evolving/)).

Mesh- and scan-to-CAD has funded entrants: Backflip raised a $30M Series A ([StartupIntros](https://startupintros.com/orgs/backflip-ai)). Incumbent MCP endpoints are appearing: Fusion MCP servers, plus a reported Onshape FeatureScript MCP server, a single-source claim ([getleo.ai](https://www.getleo.ai/blog/onshape-built-in-ai)). They make "frontier model drives your CAD tool" a commodity. Spectral, whose own product is a B-rep generator, concedes that code/VLM approaches produce "the most useful outputs for engineering" but that "VLMs do not have good spatial understanding" ([Spectral](https://www.spectrallabs.ai/research/The-AI-CAD-Frontier)). The unclaimed space is **an open, kernel-neutral, intent-preserving representation that compiles to mainstream feature trees**. No vendor has an incentive to build it, and no academic dataset is licensed to anchor it.

## Recommendations for scoping a first version

Start with the **DSL, compiler and verifier**, because every successful system in the literature depends on a fast execute-and-check loop. These also stay valuable whichever model wins. Define a typed, Python-flavored IR covering these operations:

- sketch (lines, arcs, circles, splines, with constraints and named dimensions)
- extrude and revolve
- hole as a first-class feature
- fillet and chamfer via semantic edge queries
- shell
- linear and circular pattern
- mirror

Then add booleans, units and parameter equations. That covers what the field already handles plus the five operations it most needs, and it stays below the sweep/loft tier where even frontier models fail more than 65% of the time ([Text2CAD-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.18430v1)). Compile to build123d/CadQuery for local validation (`BRepCheck_Analyzer`, solid count, volume, a degrees-of-freedom report). Add a FeatureScript emitter as an export target only, given the 2,500-call annual API caps ([Onshape API limits](https://onshape-public.github.io/docs/auth/limits)). Release this layer under a permissive license. It is the most generally useful thing a solo builder can produce, and it avoids every dataset-license trap.

Build the **evaluation harness second**, before any training. Include IR, median CD and IoU on DeepCAD and Fusion360 test splits so results compare with prior work. Note that Fusion360 data is non-commercial ([LICENSE](https://github.com/AutodeskAILab/Fusion360GalleryDataset/blob/master/LICENSE.md)), so use it for evaluation only. Add a slice of Text2CAD-Bench or BenchCAD for richer features, CADTests-style requirement tests, and your own **edit-stability metric**, which no one has published. This harness becomes the reward function later, so its speed matters. CAD-Coder's RL was 20× more expensive than its SFT almost entirely because of kernel execution ([CAD-Coder](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/564a224e88f2490f3c1deaae877d37e3-Paper-Conference.pdf)).

For **v1 generation, use an agent around a frontier model**, scoped to two inputs:

- **Text with explicit dimensions**, where frontier models already lead on human-written prompts.
- **One or more reference images or orthographic views**, the modality closest to drawings, for which cadrille shows multi-view rendering works ([cadrille](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22914)).

Follow ReliCAD's pattern rather than a bare retry loop. The model first writes a structured spec (features, dimensions, relations, order), the user confirms it, then the model generates DSL. The compiler and requirement tests return localized errors, and the agent repairs them ([ReliCAD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22325)). The "clarify before you draw" step also addresses the missing-dimensions failure that text-only systems hit ([CAD-MLLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04954v1)). Defer raster engineering drawings with GD&T, assemblies and mates, and free-form surfacing. The literature offers no working recipe for them, and RealCADBench shows even frontier agents misplacing assembly parts ([RealCADBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03773)). For mesh/scan input, use cadrille (Apache-2.0) as a baseline to compare against. Don't rebuild it; just note its mixed training-data provenance.

Treat **fine-tuning as a v2 distillation step**, triggered when agent cost or latency becomes the complaint. The ingredients will already exist:

- a **procedural generator in your own DSL**, which is commercially clean and cheap, and which beat human data in CAD-Recode
- agent traces filtered by the verifier, where quality beats quantity (8K good samples beat 70K mediocre ones in CAD-Coder)
- a reward from the harness

SFT a 2–3B Qwen-class (V)LM on one GPU, then run small-scale online RL on hard examples only, as cadrille did, before considering anything at CAD-Coder's 8-GPU, 146-hour scale ([CAD-Recode](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.14042), [CAD-Coder](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/564a224e88f2490f3c1deaae877d37e3-Paper-Conference.pdf), [cadrille v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22914v3)). Expect the model to be strong in-distribution and brittle on unseen part families ([BenchCAD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10865)). Keep the frontier agent as the fallback. Skip from-scratch generator training entirely. If a custom model earns its place, it is a narrow sketch-constraint inferencer trained with solver rewards in the AutoConstrain style ([AutoConstrain](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13178)).

## Conclusion

Over 2024–2026 the problem changed shape. It began as "can a network emit a valid CAD sequence?", a question now effectively answered for sketch+extrude parts. It has become "can a system emit a model an engineer would keep editing?", and here the field has almost no measurements. The decisive assets are therefore no longer architectures but **verifiers and representations**. The verifier is a kernel loop that checks validity, constraints, requirements and edit stability. The representation is a DSL whose references survive regeneration. Those turn any model, frontier or fine-tuned, into a better one: they serve as feedback for agents, as filters for synthetic data, and as rewards for RL.

That inverts the usual solo-builder calculus. Training compute is affordable and models are commoditizing quickly, as frontier systems leapfrog fine-tuned specialists within months on out-of-distribution prompts. Meanwhile, a permissively licensed, intent-aware DSL with an edit-stability benchmark has no current owner. Incumbents are building assistants, startups have proprietary languages, and academic datasets are locked behind non-commercial terms. A project that publishes the representation and the yardstick first, and plugs in whichever generator scores best on it, is both the most feasible scope and the most durable contribution.
