# Sourced pack data sinks the shelf bracket

Most of the numbers in `src/dipm/packs.py` now have cited sources. The mapping in the second-to-last section says which entries can be marked `placeholder: false`, which should stay provisional, and which have no data behind them. The biggest correction is about **what basis each value is on**, more than its size. The placeholders mix bases: "drywall with anchors, 200 N" is roughly a *mean ultimate* pull-out for a plastic or self-drilling anchor, while "wood stud, 800 N" sits in the *allowable* (NDS design value) band, whose ultimate is about 5.5× higher. Printed in-layer tensile strengths (PLA ≈ 50, PETG ≈ 45, ABS ≈ 33 MPa) survive almost unchanged. The across-layer ratio range has to widen from 0.4–0.6 to **0.3–0.8**, and a sustained-load (creep) derating that does not exist in the pack today has to be added. FDM dimensional error is mostly a **constant bias of about −0.1 mm with ±0.1 mm scatter**, not a symmetric ±0.15 mm band. Holes print about **0.15 mm undersize on diameter**. The ANSUR II hand medians (190 × 86 mm, computed from the public CSVs) confirm the current hand reference. Safety factors split by the quantity they protect: **4 on mean ultimate for drywall anchors**, about **5 for wood screws** (the NDS basis), and **about 4 for sustained static load on unreinforced plastic** (or 2 on a creep-derated strength). For R.c1 this changes the verdict from "fails" to "fails by an order of magnitude". No drywall anchor in the data carries the 608 N upper-screw tension at any defensible factor. The 5 mm PETG arm reaches 118 MPa in bending before any factor, against a 45 MPa strength. So the redesign is geometric (screw lever arm, arm section). Better numbers alone will not make it pass.

## Drywall anchor capacity spans 10× and the placeholder mixes bases

Ultimate tension in 1/2" (12.5 mm) gypsum board ranges from about **130 N for a light self-drilling anchor to about 1,180 N for a metal strap toggle**. Every source publishes on a different basis. Toggler publishes *ultimate* values and states "Industry standards recommend ¼ of ultimate test load" ([Toggler HWA bulletin](https://toggler.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/Metric-Technical_HWA.pdf)). Cobra publishes "safe working loads" that "represent a safety factor of 2:1 from ultimate test loads" ([Cobra FlipToggle](https://cdn.rona.ca/documents/FLIPTOGGLE-COBRA-Technicalsheet.pdf)). ITW publishes laboratory averages and tells the user to apply "appropriate safety factors" ([ITW E-Z Ancor](https://www.buildsite.com/pdf/itwbuildex/E-Z-ANCOR-Self-Drilling-Drywall-Anchors-Product-Data-2964390.pdf)). Wall Twister, L.H. Dottie and Confast all name 4:1 as the ultimate-to-allowable convention ([Wall Twister](https://cdn.adiglobaldistribution.us/pim/Original/10284/4731-Wall-Twister-Product-Data-Sheet.pdf); [LH Dottie](https://cdn.adiglobaldistribution.us/pim/Original/10059/LH-TBC82_Manufacturer_Brochure.pdf); [Confast](https://www.confast.com/wall-driller-technical-information)). Sure Wall's table is *already* allowable at SF 4, so dividing again would double-count ([Sure Wall](https://catalog.scfastening.com/Asset/Sure%20Wall.pdf)). A pack that stores one number without its basis cannot be checked correctly.

The SnapToggle data are the most explicit. In 12.5 mm board the BM5 failed at **1,059 N in tension and 1,098 N in shear**, and failure was defined as "onset of gouging of drywall material" ([Toggler SNAPTOGGLE](https://toggler.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/Metric-Technical_SNAPTOGGLE.pdf)). SnapToggles take M5/M6 or 3/16-24 machine bolts, not the #8/#10 wood screws the bracket specifies. The strongest anchor that fits a #8 is therefore the plastic folding toggle at about 640 N. Moving to 5/8" (15 mm) board raises tension by roughly 1.2–1.5×: TripleGrip #8 goes from 21 to 32 lb safe, and SnapToggle BM5 from 108 to 161 kg. Cobra adds a caveat that matters for a cantilever. Its values "are average tested loads for items flush to wall and will vary with items hung away from the wall" ([Cobra WallDriller Plus](https://cdn.rona.ca/documents/COBRA%20-%20WallDriller%20Plus%20-%20Technical%20Sheet.pdf)).

Read as a mean-ultimate tension for the plug/self-drilling class, the existing 200 N is about right. Its range should widen to **130–450 N**, and it must be labelled ultimate. Read as an allowable, it overstates capacity 2–4×. Tension governs for a shelf bracket: every expanding or self-drilling anchor in the table has a tension capacity of about a half to a third of its shear capacity. Three caveats bound how far these numbers can be trusted. The published values are manufacturer means with no stated scatter, so "low" is the lowest published mean, not a 5th percentile. No ICC-ES report for gypsum anchors turned up. Spring-wing toggles and mollies have no primary data in this research.

## Stud screws fail at 2–5 kN, about 5.5× their NDS design value

The National Design Specification (NDS, the US timber design code) gives a reference withdrawal design value for wood screws of **W = 2,850 G² D lb per inch of thread** (G is specific gravity, D is diameter). A worked example gives 95.5 lb/in for D = 0.19 in and G = 0.42 ([IAPMO ER-0262](https://forms.iapmo.org/ues_reports/reports/ER_0262.pdf)). The Wood Handbook's ultimate equation for screws in side grain, "based on reaching ultimate load in 5- to 10-min", has the same form ([Wood Handbook Ch. 8 via CED](https://www.cedengineering.com/userfiles/S03-019%20-%20Wood%20as%20An%20Engineering%20Material%20-%20Fastenings%20-%20US.pdf)). Its coefficient is **15,700** in inch-pound units ([Engineers Edge](https://engineersedge.com/calculators/wood_screw_pullout_withdraw_resistance_15385.htm)), so the design value is about 1/5.5 of mean ultimate. ICC-ES evaluations of proprietary screws use a factor of 3 on average test results ([ICC-ES ESR-3933](https://icc-es.org/wp-content/uploads/report-directory/ESR-3933.pdf)).

| Screw, lumber G | NDS reference, N/mm | Ultimate, N/mm | NDS @ 25 / 40 mm thread | Ultimate @ 25 / 40 mm |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| #8 (4.17 mm), SPF 0.42 | 14.4 | 79.5 | 360 / 577 N | 1,990 / 3,180 N |
| #10 (4.83 mm), SPF 0.42 | 16.7 | 92.2 | 418 / 669 N | 2,300 / 3,690 N |
| #8, Douglas fir 0.50 | 20.5 | 113 | 512 / 819 N | 2,820 / 4,510 N |
| #10, Douglas fir 0.50 | 23.7 | 131 | 593 / 949 N | 3,260 / 5,220 N |

Shank diameters come from the Wood Handbook's Table 8-9. The equations above give the per-mm rates, and the rest is arithmetic. The current "800 N (600–1000)" is therefore an NDS-style *allowable* for a #10 with 35–40 mm of thread in dense lumber. For an #8 in SPF with about 32 mm of thread (a 2" screw through 12.5 mm board and a 5 mm bracket), the allowable is about **460 N** and the mean ultimate about **2,500 N**. Only *threaded* penetration counts. The Wood Handbook equation assumes pilot holes of about 70% of root diameter, and end-grain withdrawal averages only 75% of side-grain. This research could not open the primary NDS text (Table 12.2B, Commentary C12.2.2), so the 2,850 constant and the 1/5 basis are confirmed only secondhand. The Eurocode 5 route gives a design value of roughly 0.7–0.9 kN for comparable inputs ([StructuralBasics](https://www.structuralbasics.com/tensile-capacity-of-screws-eurocode-tutorial/); [LNEC](https://repositorio.lnec.pt/bitstream/123456789/1006078/1/3_27_p224_poster_cruzetal.pdf)), which sits inside the NDS band. It extrapolates below its 6 mm diameter limit, though, so treat it as a sanity check only.

**Combined tension and shear.** For wood screws under combined withdrawal and lateral load, ICC-ES and IAPMO direct designers to NDS §12.4.1 ([ESR-3933](https://icc-es.org/wp-content/uploads/report-directory/ESR-3933.pdf); [ER-0262](https://forms.iapmo.org/ues_reports/reports/ER_0262.pdf)). The related linear form for sheathing fasteners is Z′α = Z′W′ / (Z′ sin α + W′ cos α) ([Missouri S&T](https://web.mst.edu/jthomas/classes/5260/concept/12/13/index.html)). The exact §12.4.1 form for wood screws was not verified. For the upper screw of this bracket the load is nearly pure withdrawal, so the interaction collapses to the withdrawal value anyway. None of the drywall-anchor makers publishes an interaction rule; they list tension and shear separately ([Toggler HWA](https://toggler.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/Metric-Technical_HWA.pdf)). In that vacuum, the conservative default for dipm is the linear rule N/N_u + V/V_u ≤ 1/SF. That is a design choice, not a sourced value. The research also flags a failure mode the pack lacks entirely: **screw-head pull-through of a thin printed leg**. It is very likely the real limit for a 5 mm bracket fixed into a stud, and Eurocode 5 treats it as a separate check.

## Printed strength holds up in-layer, but across-layer and creep dominate

In-layer printed tensile strength is well characterised. Prusament PLA tests at **51 ± 3 MPa** horizontal (ISO 527, 100% infill, 0.2 mm layers on an MK3) and PETG at **47 ± 2 MPa** ([Prusament PLA TDS](https://www.matterhackers.com/r/xFvGl8); [Prusament PETG TDS](https://www.3dprintergear.com.au/assets/files/technical-data-sheet%20(1).pdf)). Polymaker gives PolyLite PLA X-Y 52.3 MPa, PETG about 50.8 MPa and ABS **33.4 MPa** (ABS printed fan-off) ([Polymaker PLA wiki](https://wiki.polymaker.com/polymaker-products/more-about-our-products/documents/technical-data-sheets/pla/polylite-tm-pla); [PETG V5.4](https://polymaker.com/wp-content/uploads/lana-downloads/PolyLite_PETG_TDS_EN_V5.4.pdf); [ABS V5.6](https://polymaker.com/wp-content/uploads/lana-downloads/TDS_Polymaker_PolyLite-ABS_V5.6_2025-12-30_EN.pdf)). An independent study measured flat PLA at **55.5 MPa** even at 30% infill ([Eryildiz 2021](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/1580962)). Two findings widen the low ends. Polymaker's older PETG V5.2 sheet listed only **31.9 MPa** with no explanation ([V5.2](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0789/2979/files/PolyLite_PETG_TDS_V5.2.pdf)). Ahn et al. showed that raster direction alone swings FDM ABS from **10% to 73%** of moulded strength ([Ahn et al. 2002](https://iss.mech.utah.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/103/2012/10/Ahn-Anisotropic_material-2002.pdf)).

The across-layer ratio is the unstable number. Prusa's "vertical xz" column is an *on-edge* print, stronger than flat (PLA 59 MPa), not an across-layer test. Prusa's real across-layer number is its in-house "interlayer adhesion" row: **17 ± 3 MPa for PLA (ratio 0.33)** and 18 ± 4 MPa for PETG (0.38). Eryildiz measured upright PLA at 35.5 MPa (**0.64**), and Polymaker's Z columns give **0.73–0.89**. The research credits the spread to thermal history at the interface. Nozzle temperature is the strongest single driver, and part cooling and layer height also matter ([thermal-history study](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/366868722_Thermal_history_based_prediction_of_interlayer_bond_strength_in_parts_manufactured_by_material_extrusion_additive_manufacturing); [hardware factors](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/333034313_Hardware_Factors_Influencing_Interlayer_Bonding_Strength_of_Parts_Obtained_by_Fused_Filament_Fabrication)). Default PLA with the fan on sits near the low end; enclosed, fan-off ABS sits near the high end. A 0.4–0.6 band hides a real 0.3–0.8 spread. For a no-false-PASS check, the worst case of 0.3 is the honest value. The better engineering answer is to orient the part so the across-layer value never governs.

Printed flexural strength runs **1.4–1.7× tensile**: PLA 83 vs 51, PETG 66 vs 47, ABS 56 vs 33 MPa. Part of that is how ISO 178 computes stress, and part is the dense perimeters sitting at the outer fibres. Neither is guaranteed in a bracket with sparse infill. The bending check should compare σ = 6M/(bh²) with the *tensile* value, and the pack should keep the flexural value only as a reference note.

Creep has no adequate printed-part data. The longest FDM PLA creep-rupture tests found ran about **2 hours** ([Waseem et al. 2020](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7764475/)). Moulded-thermoplastic guidance puts long-term working stress at **20–40% of short-term strength**, falling to **3–5%** as service temperature approaches the heat-deflection temperature ([Protolabs](https://www.protolabs.com/resources/guides-and-trend-reports/thermoplastic-material-selection-for-injection-molding/)). PLA's HDT is only **55 °C**, against 68 °C for PETG and 98 °C for ABS. That justifies provisional sustained-load derating factors of **PLA 0.3 (0.2–0.4)** and **PETG/ABS 0.4 (0.3–0.5)**, to stay marked unreviewed until real creep-rupture data exist. A worked polycarbonate example shows the stakes: with SF 2 and long-term effects included, the allowable stress came out at about **1/12 of short-term tensile strength** ([PlasticsToday](https://www.plasticstoday.com/materials/long-term-testing-critical-for-material-selection)).

## FDM error is a −0.1 mm bias with ±0.1 mm scatter, not ±0.15

The best modern metrology is from a Bambu Lab X1-Carbon printing PLA at 0.4 mm nozzle and 0.2 mm layers, with 100 parts per size measured by CMM. Without compensation, "all XY dimensions were systematically undersized by approximately −0.12 mm across the nominal size range", with σ about 0.03–0.04 mm. Z ran about **+0.05 mm** long, and 0.8 mm walls printed at 0.75–0.78 mm ([Byczuk et al., euspen 2026](https://www.euspen.eu/knowledge-base/ICE26126.pdf)). A second X1C study found absolute deviation "approximately constant across nominal sizes", grades of **IT9–IT14**, and Z as the only plate-position-sensitive axis ([Gebre et al. 2026](https://iris.unitn.it/retrieve/aaecc8a0-0420-472b-9a40-4d43d7df5e0b/Material%20Design%20%20%20Processing%20Communications%20-%202026%20-%20Gebre%20-%20Influence%20of%20Geometry%20and%20Size%20on%20Precision%20and%20Accuracy%20in.pdf)). The two consequences for the pack are that the bias alone uses up most of a symmetric ±0.15 mm band, and that tolerances should be absolute millimetres plus a small scale term, not percentages. Per-printer calibration (+0.05 mm per side contour compensation, a +0.09% XY scale) brought every sample within IT7. The tight numbers are therefore available, but only after calibration.

Holes follow the same pattern. Vertical Ø6–10 mm holes at 0.2 mm layers printed **0.12–0.24 mm undersize** on diameter, flat across sizes, with no significant difference across five printer and slicer sites. At 0.32 mm layers the undersize reached −0.33 mm. A near-horizontal Ø2.3 hole printed at about Ø2.05 ([Popescu et al. 2023](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/applsci/applsci-13-00041/article_deploy/applsci-13-00041.pdf)). On the X1C, the diameter deviations of the cylindrical features averaged −0.134 mm vertical and −0.108 mm horizontal (Gebre, above). It was not confirmed whether those features were holes or bosses. Centre-to-centre position has no modern CMM study. Because the contour shrink cancels between two features, the residual error is axis scale (about 0.1–0.15%) plus scatter, so **±0.1 mm + 0.15% of distance** is an inference, not a measurement.

The remaining process entries rest on slicer conventions and design guides. PrusaSlicer's automatic extrusion width is **1.125 × nozzle** ([Prusa KB](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/creating-profiles-for-different-nozzles_127540)). OrcaSlicer recommends 105–120% for outer walls ([Orca wiki](https://www.orcaslicer.com/wiki/print_settings/quality/quality_settings_line_width.html)), and Bambu defaults to 0.42 mm. Prusa recommends about **0.2 mm** of elephant's-foot compensation and turns it on in official profiles ([Prusa KB](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/elephant-foot-compensation_114487)), but no peer-reviewed measurement of the flare exists. Hubs gives the 45° overhang rule and says bridge sag is "to some extent always present unless the bridge is less than 5mm" ([Hubs](https://www.hubs.com/knowledge-base/how-design-parts-fdm-3d-printing/)). Jiang et al. show that the printable overhang threshold "varies substantially" with fan, speed and temperature ([Jiang et al. 2018](https://researchspace.auckland.ac.nz/handle/2292/44788)). The unsupported part of a horizontal Ø10 hole is a crown chord of about 7 mm, between Hubs' 5 mm and the roughly 20 mm "clean" span vendor guides cite ([QIDI](https://qidi3d.com/blogs/news/3d-printing-bridging-techniques-guide)). Ø10 is therefore a reasonable nominal, but it is geometric inference.

## ANSUR II confirms the hand; safety factors split by what they protect

The public ANSUR II files (4,082 men, 1,986 women; [OpenLab](https://www.openlab.psu.edu/ansur2/)) give pooled **hand length P50 190 mm (P5–P95 171–208)** and **hand breadth P50 86 mm (74–94)**. These are unweighted statistics computed from the [male](https://tools.openlab.psu.edu/publicData/ANSUR_II_MALE_Public.csv) and [female](https://tools.openlab.psu.edu/publicData/ANSUR_II_FEMALE_Public.csv) CSVs; the official weighted tables may differ by about 1 mm. By sex, the medians are men 193 × 88 and women 180 × 78. The current 190 × 85 is correct. ANSUR II has **no finger or fingertip measurements**, so the fingertip cannot be "sized from ANSUR II" as the code comment promises. The FAA Tech Ops survey gives index-finger breadth at the PIP joint (the middle knuckle) with medians of **21 mm (men) and 18 mm (women)**, and index-finger length medians of 74 and 69 mm. Its hand-breadth figures match ANSUR II within 1 mm ([FAA HFDS App. B](https://hf.tc.faa.gov/hfds/download-hfds/hfds_pdfs/App_B_Tech_Ops_Anthropometrics.pdf)). The fingertip is narrower than the PIP joint, so **18 mm** is a defensible reference breadth, up from 16. The 25 mm pad length still has no source. Greiner's 1991 Army hand survey (DTIC ADA244533) measures DIP-joint breadth and should be fetched by hand.

No standard gives a single design safety factor for a consumer wall shelf. The closest analogues are test multipliers. UL 2442 display mounts must survive **4× rated load** for displays up to 100 lb ([ULSE](https://ulse.org/insight/ul-standards-engagement-standards-matter-how-standards-wall-and-ceiling-mounts-help-prevent-serious/)). EN 16122 tests storage furniture with distributed loads of 0.65–2.5 kg/dm² rather than multipliers ([BCI test report](https://bci.dk/media/27195/829645-1-60_30-classic-steel-shelving-en-16121_16122_14073-uk.pdf)). Anchor makers use 4:1 on ultimate. Plastics design literature recommends about **2 for short-term static load and 4 for long-term static load**, preferably applied to creep data ([design criteria paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/240421046_Design_criteria_and_safety_factors_for_plastic_components_design)). A loaded shelf is a sustained load, so R.c1's SF 2 on short-term strength is unconservative for the printed body. That conclusion holds most strongly for PLA.

## Decisions only the designer can make

**Store ultimate, apply SF in the check.** The sources disagree on whether they publish ultimates (Toggler, ITW, Wood Handbook) or allowables (Cobra 2:1, Sure Wall 4:1, NDS about 1/5). If the pack stores one number per entry, it must also store the basis and the source's own factor. Otherwise a Cobra "safe" value divided again by 4 is overly conservative, and an NDS value divided by 4 again is wrongly penalised 20×. The recommendation is to store mean ultimate with `basis: "mean ultimate"` and `source_sf`, then let the check apply its factor. The NDS allowable then becomes a derived view (ultimate ÷ 5), not a stored number.

**Which factor protects which quantity.** One global SF 2 cannot be right for three failure modes with different evidence. The sourced conventions are 4 on drywall-anchor ultimate, about 5 on wood-screw ultimate, and 4 on short-term plastic strength under sustained load. The designer must also decide whether SF covers creep. Either SF 4 is applied to short-term strength, or an explicit derate is combined with SF 2, but never both: 50 × 0.3 × 0.3 / 2 ≈ 2.3 MPa double-penalises PLA. The explicit-derate route is the better fit for dipm. It makes creep a visible, revisable assumption and is more conservative for PLA (allowable 7.5 MPa vs 12.5 MPa). For PETG the two routes give similar allowables (9 vs 11.25 MPa).

**What "worst case" means.** The `lo`/`hi` values in these ranges are the minimum and maximum of published *means*. They are not 5th percentiles, and the anchor data publish no coefficient of variation. Worst-case propagation using `lo` is therefore not a characteristic value, and the report should not imply it is.

**Bias plus band versus symmetric tolerance.** `packs.py` currently states that the tolerance is "not a bias: as-made prediction is out of scope". The data say the error is mostly bias. Either dipm brings bias into scope (bias −0.10, band ±0.10, holes −0.15 on Ø), or a symmetric value must widen to **±0.2 mm** to contain the bias. Keeping ±0.15 under-reports the error. The defaults also describe a well-tuned enclosed printer (the X1C data) unless a printer class is part of the context.

**Default install class, interaction rule and proof load.** A #8 wood screw implies the plug/self-drilling class or the plastic toggle, not a strap toggle. The designer picks the default, and whether the plastic toggle is offered as a reading in the interpretations mechanism. The linear N/N_u + V/V_u rule for anchors is a chosen convention, not a sourced one. A UL-2442-style proof statement ("survives 4 × 20 kg = 80 kg") is an optional, clearer way to express the anchor and shelf requirement to a non-engineer.

## The bracket fails by about 10×, and geometry is the fix

Run the existing R.c1 model unchanged (pivot at the leg bottom, T = M·z_max/Σz², arm section 30 × 5 mm at the gusset end, PETG printed shelf-face-down so bending stress is in-layer, shelf depth 200 mm). At 20 kg it gives an upper-screw tension of **608 N** (912 N at the 30 kg range end) and an arm bending stress of **117.7 MPa** (176.5 MPa at 30 kg), both before any safety factor. The tension implies an effective screw lever arm of only about 52 mm, because §8.4a option 2 confines the screws below the gusset (gusset foot about 65 mm above the pivot).

| Fixing reading | Capacity used | SF | Utilisation at 20 kg (30 kg) | Rated load that passes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Current placeholder | 200 N | 2 | 6.1 (9.1) | ~3 kg |
| Drywall plug/self-drilling | 250 N ult (130 lo) | 4 | **9.7 (14.6)**; 18.7 at lo | ~2 kg |
| Plastic folding toggle | 640 N ult | 4 | 3.8 (5.7) | ~5 kg |
| Metal strap toggle (needs machine bolt) | 1,000 N ult | 4 | 2.4 (3.6) | ~8 kg |
| #8 into SPF stud, ~32 mm thread | 2,540 N ult | 5 | 1.20 (1.80) | ~17 kg |
| #10 into Douglas fir stud, ~32 mm thread | 4,190 N ult | 5 | 0.73 (1.09) | ~28 kg |

The arm is worse. Even with no factor, 117.7 MPa is 2.6× PETG's 45 MPa in-layer strength and 1.8× its 66 MPa flexural strength. With width tied to thickness (b = 6t), σ ≈ M/t³. The thickness needed at 20 kg is about **8.6 mm** at SF 2 on short-term strength (the current rule), **10.7 mm** at SF 4, and **11.4 mm** at SF 2 with the PETG 0.4 sustained derate. All three lie outside the declared range t ∈ [4, 8]. Even at t = 8 the arm reaches 27.6 MPa, which fails the current SF 2 rule. Printing upright (stress across layers, ratio 0.3 at worst) would cut the strength by up to a further 3×.

What the bracket needs is a geometric change, not a better pack value. The arm needs a deeper section (a rib or deeper gusset carried to the tip) rather than a thicker plate. The wall pattern needs a longer lever arm: a single upper fixing would have to sit about 126 mm above the pivot for a strap toggle at SF 4, and about 63 mm for a 500 N stud allowable. In practice the bracket needs a stud mount with #10 screws, a tension-only drywall reading cannot be made to work at 20 kg, and the R.c1 statement should spell out that anchors in plain drywall cap this bracket at a few kilograms. Head pull-through, leg bending and gusset stresses are still unchecked and could govern once the arm and anchors are fixed.

## Conclusion

Swapping in sourced numbers did not move any value very far. The work that mattered was making each number say *what it is*: a mean ultimate or an allowable, a bias or a scatter band, a short-term or a sustained strength, measured on which printer. A pack entry needs `basis`, `source_sf`, `conditions` and a confidence or provisional status as first-class fields, or the same 200 N will keep meaning different things to different checks. That is the pack-data form of the project's "no false PASS" priority. The worked recheck shows the second lesson. Under any honest reading, R.c1's failure is structural. The model already predicted it, and the old placeholders only understated its size. The next useful step is to fetch the three primary texts that would turn the remaining provisional entries into sourced ones: the NDS Chapter 12 tables and commentary, Greiner's 1991 hand survey for DIP-joint breadth, and any printed-PETG/PLA creep-rupture curve. Alongside that, add head pull-through and a sustained-load term to the R.c1 model itself.
